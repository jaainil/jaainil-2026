**Jainil & Hetal**

**Our Story**

_From strangers, to friends, to "us."_

May 13, 2026 — present

# Chapter One — "You're now friends. Say hi!"

_May 13, 2026_

It started the most ordinary way two people can start — with someone else putting them in a group and typing, "You're now friends. Say hi!" Neither Jainil nor Hetal picked this. A mutual friend did. Within minutes, Jainil was sending her a Family Guy fun-fact reel, and by the end of that first hour the two of them had traded a dozen memes, reels, and reactions back and forth — movie trivia, comedy clips, the occasional "🤣" and "😂." It was nothing more than two strangers being introduced. But it was the first thread of something that neither of them expected to keep pulling.

# Chapter Two — Getting Used to Each Other

_Mid-to-late May 2026_

What followed was days of ordinary, easy conversation. They talked about college, about clinic days and "waha ke politics," about diets and gym trainers, favourite chocolates (Hetal: Melody, Amul dark, and the Dairy Milk crackers), old shows, and whatever reel had made them laugh that hour. Jainil admitted once that when he talks to other girls he sometimes has to lean on AI just to keep the conversation going — but with Hetal, he never had to try. "Muje nahi nikalne padte aapse baat karne ke liye," he told her. "You are different."

Underneath the jokes, something was quietly shifting. Jainil started teasing about "chori chipe milne aana" — sneaking over to see her — and Hetal played along, half daring him: "chori krne aa jao clinic pe." It was banter. But banter that both of them kept coming back to.

# Chapter Three — The Clinic

_Late May 2026_

And then he actually went.

Somewhere in the middle of all that teasing, the joke stopped being a joke, and Jainil showed up at the clinic where Hetal worked — in person, for the first time. Months later, thinking back on that exact moment, he would put it into words far better than any retelling could:

_"I still remember when I first went to the clinic… and then my soul saw you, and it kinda went, 'oh… there you are. I've been looking for you.' (kind of pehli nazar wala pyaar) I didn't know it that day, but somehow you became someone so special to me."_

He didn't say a word about it to her that day. He just looked — and left already a little bit hers, without fully realizing it yet. Hetal, on her end, was far from unaffected. When the truth finally came out between them days later, she confessed she'd known exactly what was happening the moment he'd walked in: "I knew it jab aap aaye the… that's why I was fkn nervous." Two people, both quietly rattled by one visit, both saying nothing about it — for now.

# Chapter Four — Circling Closer

_May 23–25, 2026_

The days after were full of small, charged moments disguised as jokes. Jainil, only half-joking, asked if there was anything worth "ghumne layak" near her clinic — "matlab kabhi aaya to." He told her, almost too casually, "You look beautiful today — dekha nahi par pata hai." On May 25th, in the middle of a completely different conversation, he suddenly told her something that had clearly been sitting with him for a while: "Matlab maine to aapko pehli baar dekha tha uss din… dekha hoga par pata nahi tha ke aap itni pyaari dikhti ho, nahi to seedha school se try karta." The pull between them was no longer subtle. It just hadn't been said out loud yet.

# Chapter Five — May 26, 2026: "I genuinely like you"

_May 26, 2026, evening_

It happened on a Tuesday evening. Jainil had spent the day telling himself he'd wait, that he'd ask her in person, offline, when the moment felt right. He couldn't wait anymore.

_"f it i was thinking ke offline puchunga but naah i can't wait… I genuinely like you. Umm kind of more than like but I don't know how to explain… but yaa i need you okay. Amm anyway i will respect whatever you say so yaa thats it."_

He sent it, then immediately followed up, half bracing for silence: "Amm no pressure at all this is just how i feel about you." Minutes crawled by. Then Hetal answered — nervous, honest, unwilling to pretend otherwise:

_"Ok I also like you kinda idk what 😂 but yeah we can give it a try but i might fear like idk…"_

What followed wasn't a clean, cinematic yes — it was something more real. Hetal admitted she was scared: of her past, of getting too involved and having their families say no, of being hurt again. Jainil didn't brush any of it aside. He told her he'd promised her over-protective best friend Priya — the one person who knew Hetal well enough to be consulted first — that he'd never repeat whatever had hurt her before. He told her, plainly, "I can tell you I am not going to leave you. Forever."

Slowly, between the fear and the reassurance, they arrived at each other.

_"Well so the moral of the story is we both like each other."_

Hetal, always the one to keep him grounded, shot back: "We can give it try but wo dar to rehta h na." And Jainil, only half-joking again, answered with something that turned out to be more prophetic than he knew: "i hope you get it what i am trying to say — it will be a great story to tell our kids."

It was Hetal who quietly filled in the last piece of the puzzle that evening — that she hadn't just liked him from that one visit to the clinic. "The moment you made this account and I can say long before," she told him, when he asked if she'd known all along. Whatever had started as a joke about "chori" visits and clinic banter had, without either of them fully noticing, become real for both of them — possibly for a while.

By the end of that conversation, they had agreed to try — quietly, nervously, in their own unglamorous way. The actual words "boyfriend" and "I love you" were still a few days away.

# Chapter Six — May 31, 2026: "Will You Be My Boyfriend?"

_May 31, 2026, morning_

May 26th had been the nervous, honest "we both like each other" — the day they agreed to try. But the words that made it fully real came five days later, on a Sunday morning, in the middle of absolutely nothing serious.

They were just joking around — Jainil going on with some silly bit about becoming a "maa" because of a paani puri mishap, Hetal laughing along — when he suddenly stopped mid-joke.

_"Anyways jokes aside i wanted to tell you — I love you ❤️"_

Hetal went shy immediately. Instead of answering straight away, she turned it back on him: "Okay jokes apart ask me do you love me." So he did — "Do you?" — and she gave him the simplest, truest answer she had: "Yes, I do 💗," before admitting, "Iska reply dena sharm aa raha hai, so yeah."

For a few minutes they just sat in it — virtual hugs, a "felt ✅," Hetal saying quietly, "Honestly it feels right with you, whatever jo bhi hai but haa sahi hai." Then, still laughing, still a little shy, she was the one who asked the real question — in her own words, using her own silly nickname for the moment:

_"Will you be my boyfriend? Sleepy pokemon 💤"_

Jainil made her wait exactly one beat — "Ooo let me think 🤔" — before answering with everything he had: "Yes freaking yes in every universe." Hetal's reply came instantly: "Hehehe so yeah I love you too 💖." And just like that, "boyfriend" and "girlfriend" stopped being words they were dancing around and became the actual, official truth. Jainil couldn't resist the line he'd clearly been saving: "Well now finally i can use — I love you 3000."

# Chapter Seven — Learning Each Other

_Late May – early June 2026_

The days after were softer. Jainil, by his own admission, had no experience being in a relationship and asked her more than once to be patient with him — "lack of experience, i don't know how to like be in relationships, so if i do something please nausikhiya samaj ke maf kar dena." Hetal, in turn, kept reminding him she wasn't going anywhere, even as her own nerves surfaced again and again. Good mornings turned into "I love you"s far sooner than either of them probably planned. Small jokes about marriage — "I want marry you and have great life," "abto jamai bane wale hai" — started slipping into completely unrelated conversations, the way they do when two people have quietly decided this is the real thing.

# Chapter Eight — June 6, 2026: Getting Louder About It

_June 6, 2026_

By early June, "I love you" had stopped being a big, nervous confession and had become something they said constantly, easily, in the middle of completely ordinary days — while one of them was stuck in traffic, mid-argument about a stranger's bad behaviour, or just before falling asleep. On the evening of June 6th, in the middle of talking about something else entirely, Jainil suddenly told her: "Main bada ganda hu express karne main par yaar sachi main bada pyaar karta hu tere se." Hetal's answer was simple — "Koi na, pata hai maine" — and then, that night, unprompted: "I want to marry you and have a great life." It wasn't a proposal. It was just two people, less than two weeks into knowing they liked each other, already imagining a lifetime.

# Chapter Nine — Looking Back, Together

_August 23, 2026_

Almost three months later, on an ordinary morning full of reels and inside jokes, Jainil sat down and wrote Hetal a long, unguarded message — the kind that only makes sense once you already know how the story started.

_"Hetal, my love — I still remember when I first went to the clinic… and then my soul saw you… I didn't know it that day, but somehow you became someone so special to me. And now when I look back, it honestly feels like maybe my heart already knew something that I didn't."_

_"Because I don't want a perfect love story. I just want our story. I wanna keep making memories with you, keep annoying you, keep hugging you too tightly, keep calling you my cute monkey, keep making you smile, and most importantly… I wanna keep being yours."_

_"And if I had to go back to that first day at the clinic and choose all over again… I'd still choose you. Every single time."_

Hetal's reply, as always, was simple, warm, and completely hers: "I love you too myyy babyyy."

# Epilogue — Still Going

_Present day_

What began as "You're now friends, say hi!" turned into months of reels and inside jokes, a boy who kept finding excuses to walk into a clinic he had no real reason to visit, a girl who noticed him the moment he did and was too nervous to say so, and a nervous, unpolished, very honest "I genuinely like you" sent on a Tuesday evening because one of them simply couldn't wait any longer to say it.

They still bicker about small things, still send each other reels at 2 a.m., still joke about "saas" and "nand" and married life long before either of them is actually married, and still end almost every day exactly the way they've ended it since that May evening — with an "I love you" that, by now, isn't nervous at all.

_— Their story, still being written. —_
