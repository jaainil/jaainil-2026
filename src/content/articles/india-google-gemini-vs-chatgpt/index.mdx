Google is doing almost everything you'd expect a company with unlimited distribution power to do to make Gemini huge in India.

Bundle it with Jio.

Give people premium AI access.

Throw in terabytes of Google Drive, Gmail and Photos storage.

Put Gemini inside the Google ecosystem people already use every day.

Run a massive advertising campaign.

And when that still isn't enough attention, bring in Shah Rukh Khan.

You can't accuse Google of being subtle.

And yet, there's something I keep noticing.

From school friends to college friends to people I now meet at work, whenever AI tools come up in conversation, I keep hearing versions of the same thing:

"I use ChatGPT."

"I got Gemini Pro free, but I mostly wanted the storage."

"I use Claude sometimes."

"Gemini? Yeah, I have it."

Having Gemini and *choosing Gemini* are two very different things.

Before somebody starts typing an angry comment, no, I'm not claiming to have conducted a scientific survey of 1.4 billion Indians. My friends, colleagues and acquaintances are an anecdotal sample. They cannot prove that "India hates Gemini."

Gemini clearly has plenty of satisfied users. Its [Android app listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.google.android.apps.bard) includes positive reviews, and there are users who actively prefer Gemini over ChatGPT. Ratings and reviews can change, and neither is a representative survey.

But my observation raises a much more interesting question:

**Can Google manufacture AI adoption through distribution, bundles and advertising if users simply enjoy talking to another AI more?**

That may become one of the most important consumer-AI battles in India.

## Google's India strategy is actually extremely smart

Let's give Google credit before attacking it.

The Jio partnership is a monster distribution move.

[Google and Reliance announced in October 2025](https://blog.google/company-news/inside-google/around-the-globe/google-asia/reliance-jio-india-partnership/) that eligible Jio users would receive Google AI Pro for 18 months at no additional cost. The original announcement included Gemini 2.5 Pro, NotebookLM, image and video tools and 2 TB of Google cloud storage.

And the offer has become even more aggressive.

As of October 2026, [Jio's Gemini offer page](https://www.jio.com/google-gemini-offer/) advertises **5 TB of storage**, Gemini access, NotebookLM, Workspace integration, image tools and video generation for eligible users. Eligibility and continued access depend on the [offer's terms](https://www.jio.com/jcms/en-in/jio-google-gemini-terms-and-conditions/).

Five terabytes.

At that point you're competing on far more than chatbot intelligence.

Google is effectively saying:

Come for Gemini. Stay because your entire digital life now has enough cloud storage to survive three phone upgrades and seventeen family WhatsApp groups.

That's clever.

It also creates a strange measurement problem.

If millions of Indians activate Gemini because their Jio plan gives them several terabytes of Google storage, does that mean millions of Indians *prefer Gemini*?

Not necessarily.

Activation is not affection. Subscription is not usage. Usage is not loyalty.

And loyalty is ultimately what matters.

## The Jio bundle may accidentally reveal Gemini's biggest problem

This is where my completely unscientific friend-group research becomes interesting.

Several people I've spoken to don't describe the Jio offer as:

> "I got Gemini Pro!"

They describe it more like:

> "Bro, I got Google storage free."

Gemini becomes one feature inside a much bigger package.

And honestly? If I'm Google, that would worry me slightly.

Google wants Gemini to become the reason you enter the ecosystem.

But for some users, Gemini may currently be the free toy sitting next to the storage product they actually wanted.

That doesn't make the strategy bad. Quite the opposite. Bundling is one of Google's greatest advantages.

It simply means Google should be careful when interpreting subscriber numbers.

There's a massive difference between someone who *has access* to an AI model and someone whose brain automatically thinks:

**"I need to ask ChatGPT."**

That second behaviour is brutally difficult to steal.

## Free doesn't automatically create loyalty

People in tech sometimes underestimate this.

They assume consumers will naturally use whichever product gives them the most features for the lowest price.

Humans don't behave like benchmark spreadsheets.

WhatsApp isn't dominant in India because everybody wakes up every morning comparing messaging protocols.

Google Search didn't become a verb because consumers continuously evaluated search-engine specifications.

Products win when behaviour becomes automatic.

And ChatGPT appears to have achieved an unusually powerful version of that.

[OpenAI said in February 2026](https://openai.com/index/openai-for-india/) that India had surpassed **100 million weekly ChatGPT users**. That's a company-reported figure rather than an independent market census, but even with that caveat, it demonstrates the enormous scale ChatGPT has already reached in the country.

Google therefore isn't entering an empty market. It's trying to overwrite an existing habit.

Interestingly, OpenAI has also played the free-distribution game. In November 2025, eligible Indian users could redeem 12 months of ChatGPT Go at no cost. That promotion ended on January 21, 2026, according to the [ChatGPT release notes](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/6825453-chatgpt-release-notes).

Both companies understand that India is a market where getting millions of people into the habit matters enormously.

The difficult part comes after the promotion.

**Which app do people open when nobody is bribing them with free stuff anymore?**

That's the real test.

## Why does ChatGPT feel different?

Here's where benchmarking becomes almost useless.

You can tell me Model A scored 87.4 on Benchmark XYZ while Model B scored 86.9.

Cool.

I'm opening the app because I need to explain to an AI why my Kubernetes deployment has decided to ruin my Tuesday.

Consumer AI is about interaction as much as raw intelligence.

ChatGPT often feels like you're continuing a conversation.

Gemini can sometimes feel like you've approached an extremely knowledgeable employee at a service counter.

Both may eventually give you the information. The emotional experience is different.

And yes, "emotional experience" sounds ridiculously dramatic when we're discussing chatbots.

But that's exactly the product category these companies created.

When your interface is literally a conversation, personality becomes UX. Tone becomes UX. Memory becomes UX.

How aggressively the assistant restates your question becomes UX.

Whether it understands what you *meant* instead of merely parsing what you *said* becomes UX.

The best AI model on paper can still lose if people dislike interacting with it.

## "Gemini sounds robotic" is more important than Google may think

This is probably the complaint I hear most.

The criticism is that it sometimes **feels like a robot**, even when the answer is useful.

There's a particular style of AI answer everyone recognizes.

"Certainly! I'd be happy to assist you with that."

Then three headings. Then six bullet points. Then a conclusion nobody asked for.

Technically correct. Emotionally dead.

ChatGPT can absolutely fall into the same trap, and Claude has its own personality quirks. None of these products are magically immune to generic AI writing.

But conversational feel matters disproportionately because users spend hundreds or thousands of interactions training themselves to expect a certain rhythm.

Once someone becomes comfortable thinking aloud with one assistant, moving to another assistant can feel weird even when the second model is objectively capable.

That's why AI switching costs are becoming psychological rather than technical.

Your conversations become context. Your prompts become habits. Your preferred response style becomes expected behaviour.

Eventually the assistant stops feeling like software you're testing and starts feeling like *your AI*.

That's the territory Google needs to win.

## Gemini does have personalization now

It would be inaccurate to say Gemini has no memory or personalization.

Google has invested heavily in exactly that.

[Gemini can use past chats for personalization](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16598469?hl=en), subject to feature availability and settings. Google also [launched Personal Intelligence in India on April 14, 2026](https://blog.google/intl/en-in/products/personal-intelligence-launches-in-the-gemini-app-in-india/), allowing users to connect information from supported Google services.

That is an enormous theoretical advantage.

Google probably holds more useful information about many Android users than a standalone AI company could access, assuming the user chooses to connect those services.

Meanwhile, ChatGPT also offers personalization through features such as [memory](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8590148-memory-faq), when available and enabled.

So this is no longer a simple feature checklist where ChatGPT has memory and Gemini doesn't.

The question is:

**Which product makes that personalization feel useful, consistent and natural?**

And this is where my experience, and the experience of people I talk to, still tends to favour ChatGPT.

Google's personalization also has some fragmentation. Its help documentation describes availability requirements and limitations across different Gemini experiences.

For normal consumers, architectural explanations don't matter. They don't care why the AI forgot.

They just think:

"Why do I need to explain this again?"

That single sentence can destroy the illusion of having a personal assistant.

## The Gemini app itself needs to disappear as a source of friction

Then there's reliability.

I've heard complaints about Gemini's app behaving strangely, losing context or throwing errors.

The [Play Store listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.google.android.apps.bard) contains both praise and complaints. Individual reviews cannot tell us the percentage of users affected. Every giant consumer application has angry reviews, so saying "the Gemini app is broken" would be nonsense.

The important point is about expectations.

Google isn't some 12-person startup running servers from a Bengaluru apartment.

It owns Android. It runs Search. It operates Gmail, YouTube, Maps, Drive, Chrome and one of the largest cloud platforms on Earth.

People expect Google software to feel boringly reliable.

When a Google AI app fails, the reaction isn't "AI is experimental."

It's:

**"Bro, this is Google."**

The bar is higher because Google itself created the bar.

## Google has advantages ChatGPT should be terrified of

Google's position is actually terrifyingly strong.

Think about everything it controls.

Android. Chrome. Google Search. Gmail. Drive. Docs. Photos. YouTube. Maps. Google Cloud. Workspace. Your calendar. Possibly your phone's default assistant.

Now add Gemini.

ChatGPT has to convince you to open ChatGPT.

Google can make Gemini appear wherever you already are.

That distinction could determine the next phase of the AI war.

Google has already [expanded Gemini in Chrome to India](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/chrome/chrome-expands-india-new-zealand-canada/). Its announcement also explains how Android users can invoke Gemini while using Chrome and other apps.

This is why I wouldn't bet against Google.

ChatGPT may currently own the stronger AI *habit* among many users I encounter.

Google owns an absurd amount of the surrounding infrastructure.

If Gemini becomes "good enough" conversationally while becoming dramatically more convenient everywhere else, consumer behaviour can change very quickly.

Convenience has killed better standalone products before.

## The Shah Rukh Khan campaign is brilliant marketing

Google clearly understands that this battle is being fought well beyond developers in Bengaluru, Hyderabad or Gurgaon.

In August 2026, Google India launched a Gemini campaign featuring Shah Rukh Khan. [Campaign Brief Asia reported](https://campaignbriefasia.com/2026/08/10/google-india-makes-gemini-more-approachable-with-shah-rukh-khan-and-prasoon-joshi/) that Prasoon Joshi wrote it and Google developed it with McCann India and Nirvana Films. The campaign presents a friendly, multilingual assistant for audiences beyond English-speaking metros.

Strategically, that makes perfect sense.

SRK may be one of the few people capable of making an AI advertisement feel like a Bollywood conversation instead of an enterprise SaaS presentation.

But celebrity marketing has a limit.

Shah Rukh Khan can convince somebody to download Gemini.

He cannot sit beside them during their 47th conversation making sure the model understands them properly.

That responsibility returns to the product.

**The advertisement gets you the first prompt. The model earns the second.**

If the assistant feels cold, repetitive, unreliable or disconnected from the user, no celebrity endorsement fixes that.

Not even SRK.

And that man has survived some questionable movie scripts.

## Google may be solving the wrong adoption problem

The campaign appears designed around an important assumption:

People aren't using AI because AI still feels intimidating, inaccessible or linguistically disconnected from everyday India.

That seems plausible for part of the population.

Multilingual AI could be transformative in India.

A farmer, student, shopkeeper, homemaker or small-business owner shouldn't need excellent English to access powerful software.

Google's reach gives it an opportunity to make AI accessible in ways few companies can match.

But among people who already use AI regularly, the problem may be completely different.

They're not scared of AI. They already have an AI.

The challenge becomes persuading someone to leave the assistant they already trust.

That is much harder than getting them to try their first chatbot.

## India is too important to treat this as another app launch

India may become one of the defining consumer markets for AI.

It has a huge population of students, a gigantic developer community, an enormous Android base and millions of small businesses. It has strong demand for affordable software and multiple major languages.

It's also a market where mobile-first digital services spread quickly once the economics made sense.

The winner here potentially becomes the interface through which hundreds of millions of people learn, search, code, shop, create content, communicate with businesses and eventually operate software agents.

That makes India strategically different from a simple subscription market.

Google understands this. OpenAI clearly understands it too.

That's why we've seen India-specific products, partnerships and promotions from both companies.

And that's why merely giving away a premium subscription isn't enough.

## The real battle is habit

People keep discussing the AI race like Formula 1.

Who has the fastest model? Who has the biggest context window? Who topped which benchmark this Tuesday?

For many normal consumers, the question is simpler:

Who do you ask first?

When you're confused, which app opens automatically?

When you need an email rewritten, where do you paste it?

When your code breaks at 1:30 AM, who gets the error message?

When you're planning a trip, preparing for an interview, fixing Excel or asking an embarrassingly stupid question you don't want to ask another human, where do you go?

The company that owns that reflex has something far more valuable than a benchmark lead.

It owns **mental default status**.

For many people around me, that default currently seems to be ChatGPT.

Gemini is installed. Gemini is free. Gemini has storage. Gemini has Google behind it.

But ChatGPT gets opened.

That difference is the entire story.

## Storage may actually be Google's secret weapon

There's another possibility though.

Maybe Google doesn't need everyone to love Gemini immediately.

The Jio strategy could be playing a much longer game.

Give people enormous storage. Get their photos into Google Photos. Get their files into Drive. Let Gemini interact with Gmail. Connect the other Google services they already use.

Then gradually make Gemini the intelligence layer across all of it.

Suddenly switching assistants becomes more annoying because Gemini understands the Google ecosystem surrounding your life.

That's a much more defensible strategy than trying to beat ChatGPT purely in chatbot personality.

It also creates obvious privacy questions.

The more useful an AI becomes by connecting emails, photos, search history and personal information, the more carefully users need to understand what data is being accessed, how personalization works and which controls are enabled.

Google provides [controls around personalization and temporary chats](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/temporary-chats-privacy-controls/), but consumers shouldn't blindly connect everything simply because doing so produces a smarter answer.

The future personal assistant will probably know an uncomfortable amount about us.

Convenience and privacy are going to spend the next decade fighting over the same checkbox.

## What Google should actually fix

Google doesn't need another billboard telling AI users that Gemini exists.

We know.

It doesn't need another specification telling us Gemini has an enormous context window.

We believe you.

It doesn't necessarily need another free subscription either.

What it needs is obsessive attention to the boring parts of consumer software.

Make conversations feel natural. Make context persist predictably. Reduce friction.

Make personalization obvious without becoming creepy.

Make the mobile app feel impossibly reliable.

Stop making users think about which Gemini mode, model or experience remembers what.

And above everything:

**Make Gemini feel like one assistant.**

Google's greatest strength, having dozens of products, can easily become its greatest UX weakness.

Consumers don't want to understand the org chart. They want to ask something and get help.

## ChatGPT shouldn't become arrogant either

There's a trap on the other side.

ChatGPT currently having stronger mindshare among people I know doesn't guarantee anything.

Technology habits can change surprisingly fast.

BlackBerry looked permanent. Internet Explorer looked permanent. Yahoo looked permanent.

Google itself has spent decades teaching the technology industry that distribution plus a better product can destroy seemingly entrenched competitors.

ChatGPT therefore cannot simply rely on people liking its conversational style.

Google has ecosystem integration that OpenAI cannot easily replicate. Google can subsidize AI through other businesses and integrate Gemini directly into devices and workflows.

And unlike the early Bard era, Gemini is a capable product.

The gap increasingly comes down to product preference.

And preferences can change.

## So, does India actually hate Gemini?

No.

That headline would be fun. It would also be intellectually dishonest if taken literally.

India is far too large and diverse to summarize based on my friends, colleagues, Reddit posts or a few Play Store reviews.

Gemini clearly has enthusiastic users. Its capabilities are strong. Its Google ecosystem integration is arguably unmatched. Its Jio partnership gives it extraordinary distribution. Its multilingual strategy makes enormous sense for India.

And five terabytes of cloud storage is objectively difficult to complain about.

But I do think Google has a **preference problem**.

At least among many AI users I encounter, Gemini still feels like something Google is trying very hard to make them use.

ChatGPT feels like something they decided to use themselves.

That difference sounds small. It isn't.

Consumer technology is full of products that won distribution while losing affection.

And when the product itself is supposed to become the assistant you talk to every day, affection matters more than ever.

Google can buy advertisements, bundle subscriptions, give away storage and put Shah Rukh Khan on the screen.

Google can put Gemini into Android, Search, Chrome, Gmail and probably your refrigerator eventually.

But there's one thing Google cannot bundle:

**habit.**

It has to earn that.

And right now, that may be the hardest part of the entire AI race.

## Key takeaways

"India hates Gemini" is too broad to defend, but there is a product argument underneath it. Google has built an aggressive India strategy through Jio, cloud storage, ecosystem integration, multilingual features and celebrity advertising. Yet access does not automatically translate into preference.

ChatGPT already has significant behavioural momentum in India, with OpenAI reporting more than 100 million weekly users in the country by February 2026. Gemini's long-term advantage is Google's ecosystem; ChatGPT's advantage is the conversational habit it has already established with many users.

The winner will probably be determined less by benchmarks than by which assistant becomes the first place people instinctively go for help.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is ChatGPT more popular than Gemini in India?

There isn't enough independent public data cited here to make a definitive nationwide claim about active-user preference across both products. OpenAI said India exceeded 100 million weekly ChatGPT users in February 2026, while Google is expanding Gemini through Android, Chrome, Jio and its broader ecosystem.

### Is Gemini free for Jio users?

Eligible Jio users can receive Google AI Pro through the Jio Gemini offer. As of publication, Jio advertises 18 months of access along with Gemini features and 5 TB of Google storage, subject to plan eligibility and the offer's terms.

### Why might someone choose ChatGPT instead of Gemini?

Reasons can include conversational preference, familiarity, existing chat history, personalization, workflow features and simply having built a habit around ChatGPT. These are subjective factors and vary between users.

### Why might someone choose Gemini instead of ChatGPT?

Gemini has advantages for people deeply invested in Google's ecosystem. Supported connections with Google services can make it useful, while Google's Jio partnership adds substantial subscription value in India.

### Does Gemini have memory and personalization?

Yes. Gemini can personalize responses using past conversations and supported connected Google services when the appropriate features and settings are available. Google launched Personal Intelligence in India in April 2026.

### Is Gemini actually bad?

No. Both are capable AI systems with different strengths. The more interesting question is which product users prefer interacting with repeatedly and which ecosystem offers them more value.

### Will Google eventually beat ChatGPT in India?

Possibly. Google has a strong distribution advantage because it controls products Indians already use constantly. But distribution alone won't guarantee preference. Gemini still needs to turn ecosystem exposure into habitual, voluntary usage.

## Which AI do you open first?

Have you actually switched from ChatGPT to Gemini, or did you activate Gemini because your Jio plan gave you the storage?

I'm especially curious about people who use both every week.

Forget benchmarks for a second.

**When you have a random question and instinctively grab your phone, which AI do you open first?**

That answer might tell us more about the future of AI in India than another leaderboard ever will.
